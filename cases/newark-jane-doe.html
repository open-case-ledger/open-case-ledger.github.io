Newark, California · Found October 24, 1985

Newark Jane Doe

The FBI’s ViCAP materials list this woman as unidentified under Newark Police case 85-07156 and NamUs UP53306. Her nearly skeletal remains were found on October 24, 1985 in a field beside Mowry Avenue in Newark, approximately two miles from Interstate 880. Investigators believe she had been shot and may have died two months to one year before discovery.

The FBI estimates that she was 25–36 years old and 5 feet 6 inches to 5 feet 8 inches tall, with brown, red, or auburn shoulder-length hair. Her lower two front teeth had been missing for a substantial period before death, and the adjacent teeth had shifted together. Recovered items included red-orange dangling glass earrings, a Timex watch with a black strap, two distinctive white-metal rings, and two yellow plastic barrettes.

DNA Doe Project currently lists Mowry Wetlands Jane Doe as an active case. Its case page, last updated March 23, 2026, identifies Newark Police Detective Andrew Musantry as the agency contact. In a 2022 KTVU report based on interviews with then-lead Detective Todd Nobbe, investigators said the body had been cremated but Polaroid photographs of the skull and a clump of rootless hair remained in evidence. Astrea Forensics recovered DNA from that hair, allowing investigative genetic genealogy to trace a maternal family line.

The genealogy work led investigators toward southwest Missouri near the Kansas border and a close maternal relative whose DNA was consistent with a likely half-sibling relationship. Newark investigators publicly described adoption as a major obstacle to identifying Jane Doe. A 2024 Lamar Police Department statement, republished by a local news source, said the DNA-genealogy work linked the victim to the Lamar, Missouri area as a descendant of Marian Marie Richardson and that she was likely placed for adoption as an infant in the late 1940s. Open Case Ledger is treating that Lamar statement as a later public genealogy lead, not as proof of Jane Doe’s legal identity.

Open Case Ledger retired its earlier plan to ask Newark Police broad questions about whether the agency’s public case information remains current. On August 11, 2026, OCL instead requested one primary record from the department: the releasable portion of the original incident/recovery report for case 85-07156. After no response appeared in the project mailbox, OCL sent a brief same-thread status follow-up on November 17, 2026. That report may preserve more precise recovery-location, property, discovery, or scene information that can be compared with the existing public record.

Current research focus: await and review the original incident/recovery report; trace the documented Missouri adoption and training-school archival lanes without contacting living relatives; preserve source-specific differences in age and postmortem estimates; and avoid publishing crowdsourced identity matches unless they are independently verified by responsible agencies.
