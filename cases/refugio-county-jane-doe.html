Refugio County, Texas · Found March 2, 1992

Refugio County Jane Doe

Texas DPS says a pipeline crew found the unidentified woman on March 2, 1992 on a ranch along FM 2678, about four miles east of Refugio. She died from a gunshot wound. DPS has described her as a white or Hispanic woman, approximately 20–35 years old and about 5 feet 3 inches tall, whose remains may have been at the location for one to three years. A 2020 anthropological analysis supported an updated facial reconstruction. She remains unidentified.

Major forensic update: A Texas DPS basic-information release received by Open Case Ledger in August 2026 includes supplemental report 2024-0667870-00174. The report states that on March 27, 2026, Company D Texas Ranger Nathan Mutz received a University of North Texas Center for Human Identification Forensic Genetic Genealogy Laboratory Report. The supplement says FGG testing had been conducted on the unidentified remains and that the resulting genetic data was eligible to be entered and searched in the GEDmatch Pro database.

What remains unknown: The supplement says search results and further recommendations would be provided in a subsequent report. That later report was not part of the released basic-information packet. The supplement also names an attachment, Kintelligence Report F-2585.pdf, which was not included in the release. Open Case Ledger therefore does not know whether GEDmatch Pro searching produced useful matches, what recommendations followed, or what the named Kintelligence report contains.

Records status: Texas DPS released the basic information at no cost but withheld the remainder of the incident report, photographs, video, and witness statements under Texas Government Code § 552.108(a)(1), citing the open or pending criminal investigation. The released supplement states that the investigation will continue.

Why this changes the public baseline: DPS's June 10, 2026 public appeal said investigators were exploring forensic testing. The released March 27 supplemental report shows that forensic genetic genealogy had already progressed to completed laboratory testing and a data set eligible for GEDmatch Pro searching. That does not mean an identification is imminent, but it materially narrows the question from whether FGG has been attempted to what the search produced.
